They said each shuttle would contain six\
to eight persons. But hold on, we're not astronauts.\
Just space travelers seeking weightlessness.\
Every man you can imagine on this ship is carrying\
something too heavy to withstand. & for\
the majority, it is grief heaped upon grief\
heaped upon grief. We had bags of food & water.\
Cameras to catch a fleeting glimpse of our smiles.\
Spacesuits & diaries. & yes, a bag of memories\
to forget. When the flight attendant asked us\
to move toward the bag and empty those memories\
we do not ever want to have again, I shoved\
my hand into my pocket & fumbled for the note\
she left on the table the day she eloped with\
sickle cell anemia. That one guy that had always\
been there breaking her from skin to blood\
to bone marrow. & she, the loyal dog in the toxic\
relationship, subjected herself to the excruciating\
pain & suffering. If she were here, we would\
have traveled together in the summer shuttle to see\
the sun. Our bodies, empty & under zero gravity\
floating in the midair like weaverbirds & canaries.\
The power of this *weightlessness* is how it makes\
you unbreakable, unfazed even when you're stand-\
ing in the zenith, face to face with the sun—right\
there refusing to burn because you want to be cooked\
& not charred—tender & not ash. “Excuse me sir,\
do you have any memory you would like to dispose\
of?” “Oh, yes, I do. I do.” So I looked in the bag\
& tossed the note away, then walked to my seat,\
tightened my seat belt & wondered if all this while\
you were the silence that kept recurring at the\
end of my songs or the sweet melody within,\
like the consonant of an alliteration.
