The surface tension of our talk\
awkwardly ended in silence on

the island after we’d kayaked.\
I’d yakked on about nothing,

thinking you’d forget how,\
out on the water, I brought up your

horror of lurking sharks,\
far from the thoughtful guy

I think you thought I was.\
Then I saw the perfect stone,

honed by waves and centuries,\
and gently handed it to you.

You knew what to do with it.\
If it had skipped just once,

bounced off the surface in\
continuing its path,

as if a sentence we fished\
with to describe the water,

or several times, like ellipses, those\
hopeful omissions . . .

a son, an old age together . . .\
perhaps we could have flown

home to try again. But it flew\
through wind we stood in, tossed out,

and we watched it break the surface\
first, then sink into the depths.
